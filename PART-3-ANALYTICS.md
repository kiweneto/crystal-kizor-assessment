# Part 3 — Analytics & Improvement (under 250 words)

I would measure whether the site helps the right visitors understand the brand and complete a relevant next step—not just whether traffic increases.

**What I would track:** acquisition source and landing page; engaged sessions and scroll depth; clicks on each initiative and audience pathway; contact-button clicks; form starts, validation errors and successful submissions; device, browser and page performance. I would define an enquiry conversion rate as completed enquiries divided by eligible website sessions, and segment it by source and device.

**Tools:** Google Analytics 4 for events and traffic, Google Search Console for organic search, and PageSpeed Insights for performance. A privacy-conscious heatmap or session-recording tool could be considered only with appropriate consent and data controls.

**Scenario: 5,000 visitors, five enquiries.** That is a 0.1% enquiry rate. First I would verify analytics and confirm that the five enquiries reflect all real submissions. Then I would segment the funnel by mobile/desktop, traffic source, landing page and new/returning visitors. I would test the contact journey on real phones: visibility of the call to action, broken links, form errors, load speed, trust signals and whether each initiative has a clear route to contact. I would review search terms and campaign promises to see whether visitors match the intended audience.

Next, I would prioritise the largest evidenced drop-off, form a measurable hypothesis, and run one change at a time—for example, a clearer audience-specific CTA or a shorter enquiry form. I would compare conversion and lead quality before and after, while monitoring performance and accessibility. I would not assume the design is the cause until tracking and audience quality were checked.
